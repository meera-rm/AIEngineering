## Overview

This article supports architecture consultations where the customer wants advice
about scalability, server efficiency, load balancing, deployment approach, or
overall reliability. The article defines the discovery inputs needed before a
specialist can recommend a design direction. It is intentionally narrower than
a full architecture proposal.

## What the customer provides

The customer should provide a short description of the application or service,
its main traffic pattern, expected peak load, and whether work is primarily
interactive, scheduled, or batch-driven. Support also needs to know how
sessions are handled, whether customers require geographic failover, and which
parts of the current deployment cause the most concern. If the customer has
deployment restrictions, such as limited maintenance windows or approval gates,
those constraints should be listed early.

For load-balancing discussions, useful inputs include current entry points,
whether traffic must remain sticky, and any health-check behavior already in
place. For deployment reviews, ask how releases are performed today, how often
they happen, and what rollback capability already exists.

## What support provides

Support uses the discovery inputs to frame the consultation, identify the main
tradeoffs to evaluate, and list which architecture topics are in scope for the
first session. Typical outputs from discovery are a clarified problem
statement, a set of design questions to answer, and a recommendation on whether
the next session should focus on scaling strategy, resiliency, or deployment
workflow. Support may explain concepts such as redundancy, balancing methods,
and release isolation, but it does not finalize a customer-specific topology in
the intake stage.

## Limits

Discovery does not select or approve an environment-specific solution. Its
outputs are the questions and constraints a specialist must evaluate, not
implementation deliverables. Escalate only when the customer indicates a
current production instability that needs incident handling rather than a
planned architecture consultation.
