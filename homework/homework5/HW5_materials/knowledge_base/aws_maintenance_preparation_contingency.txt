## Overview

Use this article when a customer asks how to prepare for a scheduled AWS
Management Service maintenance event and wants practical steps to limit
disruption. The objective is readiness and contingency planning, not risky
last-minute architecture changes.

## What the customer should do before the window

Customers should review whether any planned deployments, scaling changes, or
configuration edits can be moved outside the maintenance window. For event
`AMS-2023-Q4-NET-17`, support recommends pausing non-urgent management changes
starting six hours before the scheduled start. The customer should confirm their
primary and backup technical contacts, make sure on-call staff know the event
ID and UTC timing, and save current runbooks where the team can reach them
quickly.

If the workload has business-critical transaction periods, the customer should
identify the expected impact if management-plane updates are delayed for up to
10 minutes. A recent read-only export of key configuration records and a
baseline capture of application health are useful because they help the team
recognize whether observed behavior during the window is new.

## What support provides

Support explains the maintenance scope, clarifies what is and is not expected to
be affected, and tells the customer how status updates will be shared. If the
customer asks about contingency planning, support may recommend staffing the
window, deferring optional changes, and confirming that customer-owned alerting
is monitored closely until the maintenance complete message is posted. Support
can also help identify which managed actions should not be queued during the
event.

## Safe boundaries

Do not advise customers to disable safeguards, rotate credentials, force
failovers, or redesign routing shortly before a planned maintenance event unless
another approved procedure explicitly requires it. The maintenance article is
not a reason to introduce high-risk changes.

## Escalation

Escalate during the event if customer workloads become unavailable, if an
interruption lasts longer than the notice predicted, or if the maintenance
updates stop for more than 45 minutes while the event is still open.
