## Overview

Use this article when a customer asks what a basic monitoring program should
cover for servers. It addresses general operating signals such as CPU, memory,
disk, service availability, and alert routing. It is operational background,
not a design for changing an environment.

## What the customer provides

The customer should identify which server groups are in scope, which alerts they
already receive, and which failures are currently detected too late. If they
have uptime targets or after-hours coverage constraints, those details are
useful for alert tuning discussions.

## What support provides

Support can explain which baseline indicators are commonly monitored, how noisy
alerts are reduced through thresholds and ownership, and why operational teams
separate symptom alerts from capacity trend reporting. Support may also suggest
collecting evidence before a deeper consultation if the customer reports an
intermittent performance issue.

## Limits

Baseline monitoring concepts do not determine which broader changes are
appropriate for a customer's environment. Selection and implementation
decisions require a separately scoped technical assessment.
