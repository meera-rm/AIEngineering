## Overview

This article supports consultation requests about setting up or improving a
ticketing system. It is meant for customers who want better request routing,
clearer prioritization, smoother upgrade handling, or easier day-to-day use for
their internal team. A useful recommendation depends on understanding the
customer's current workflow and constraints rather than starting with a generic
template.

## What the customer provides

The customer should provide the channels where requests currently arrive, the
main categories of work they want tracked, and how they want urgent versus
routine issues distinguished. If they already use approval steps, escalation
paths, or service targets, those should be shared as part of intake. For teams
that mention upgrade handling, support also needs to know whether upgrades are
approved in one queue, in a separate change flow, or outside the ticketing
system today.

The customer does not need to propose a final workflow diagram. The point of
discovery is to uncover it.

## What support provides

Support reviews the intake information and prepares a discovery worksheet that
maps request sources, queue owners, priority rules, and handoff points. During
the consultation, support helps identify where delays occur, whether the
workflow needs additional statuses, and where a simpler intake form would
improve adoption. Recommendations may cover prioritization logic, queue design,
and change-approval flow, but only after the current process is understood.

If the customer highlights ease of use or customer-support integration, support
should ask for specific friction points such as duplicate entry, unclear queue
ownership, or delayed handoff between teams. Sample forms, one week of typical
requests, and a list of must-have reports are often enough to make the first
session concrete without pretending the workflow can be redesigned from a single
email.

## Limits

A workflow discovery article does not answer architecture design questions,
server tuning, or product-specific administration steps for unrelated systems.
It also does not promise a customer-specific implementation plan before
consultation. If the customer cannot describe their current intake path at all,
support should still schedule discovery and ask them to gather one week of
sample requests before the session.
