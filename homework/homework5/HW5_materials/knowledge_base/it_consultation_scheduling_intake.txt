## When to use this intake

Use this article when a customer asks for a consultation about operational
topics such as ticketing workflows, server administration practices,
architecture planning, scalability, deployment design, or reliability. The
purpose of intake is to make the first specialist session productive. It is not
meant to solve the customer's environment by email or promise a finished design
before discovery.

## What the customer provides

The customer should provide a short description of the problem they want the
consultation to address, the systems or teams involved, any target date that is
driving urgency, and the names of the people who should attend. Helpful intake
details include whether the request is exploratory or tied to an active pain
point, the current size of the environment, and any known change restrictions.
For architecture or workflow requests, the customer should also share the
current-state summary they already have, even if it is incomplete.

The customer is not expected to arrive with a finished solution, a final
priority matrix, or a vendor selection. Missing discovery details can delay a
recommendation, but they do not prevent support from opening the consultation
track and reserving time with the right specialist.

## What support provides

Support confirms the consulting track, assigns the request to the correct
specialist group, and prepares an agenda for the first session. The agenda
identifies which discovery materials are still needed, who should bring them,
and whether the first meeting is meant for requirements gathering or for review
of already collected evidence. Standard intake includes one 45-minute discovery
session within five business days when availability is normal.

## Boundaries and escalation

Intake reserves discovery capacity but does not promise implementation work or
environment-specific recommendations. Any later deliverable must be defined
after discovery and confirmed within the purchased service scope. Escalate
intake when the customer reports an ongoing outage, regulatory deadline, or
security incident, because those situations require operational handling before
consultation planning.
