## Overview

Use this article when a customer reports that servers are occasionally slow and
wants consulting advice on better administrative practices. Before support
recommends process changes, the customer should collect a small, structured set
of read-only evidence so the consultation can focus on patterns rather than
guesswork.

## What the customer provides

Ask the customer for the date and approximate time of at least three recent
latency events, which applications or services were affected, and whether the
slowdown was visible to end users or only to administrators. Helpful evidence
includes CPU, memory, disk, and network utilization from the same period;
server role labels; recent patching or deployment activity; and whether the
problem appeared on one system or across a cluster. A brief note about business
impact, such as delayed request handling or missed job windows, helps support
prioritize the consultation.

Customers should gather evidence only through existing dashboards, operating
system counters, or exported logs. They should not change live performance
settings, restart services, or disable protections solely to produce data.

## What support provides

Support reviews the evidence package, highlights missing comparisons, and tells
the customer whether the first consultation should focus on workload patterns,
capacity management, or operational process. Support may ask for one additional
peak-time sample if the supplied metrics do not align with the reported slow
window. The specialist then uses the collected evidence to discuss best
practices for inspections, alerting coverage, and routine management.

Support should encourage the customer to compare one slow period with one normal
period whenever possible. That contrast often shows whether the issue is tied to
resource saturation, scheduled jobs, or an external dependency. If no baseline
exists, the customer can still capture future timestamps and exported counters;
they should not stress-test live systems merely to create a data sample for the
consultation.

## Escalation

This preparation article is for intermittent latency, not a live outage. If the
customer reports an active service interruption lasting more than 15 minutes, no
current access to core systems, or repeated crashes, route the case to the
operations response team before continuing consultation planning.
