## Overview

Use this article when a customer wants a formal review of AWS Management Service
charges because the billed total does not line up with prior projections,
expected usage, or earlier correspondence. A billing review covers managed
service line items, mapped usage metrics, and written explanations for charges
that appear unexpected. It does not decide payment method issues, provide raw
cloud-provider invoices, or approve discounts by itself.

## What the customer provides

To open the review, the customer should provide the managed-service account
number, the billing period in question, the statement or invoice date, and at
least two examples of the charges they want examined. Helpful evidence includes
their internal projection, any earlier rate guidance they received, and the
name of a backup contact who can answer follow-up questions. If the customer
has already identified specific dates with unusual usage, those dates should be
included so support can focus the comparison.

The customer is not expected to calculate the root cause or supply a corrected
bill. Missing examples may slow the review, but they do not prevent support
from acknowledging the request and collecting the rest of the evidence.

## What support provides

Support opens a billing review case, confirms the review period, and prepares a
charge-by-charge breakdown from the managed-service records for that period.
The standard output is a written summary that maps each disputed line item to
the recorded service activity, plus a note for any entry that needs deeper
finance review. When the question concerns projected versus actual usage,
support compares the agreed estimate category with the measured usage pattern
rather than the customer's internal budget alone.

For routine reviews, the first written breakdown is normally issued within
three business days. If any charge must be checked against archived service
logs, support tells the customer which entries remain under investigation and
when the next update will be sent.

## Limits and escalation

A billing review explains charges and identifies whether a separate adjustment
decision is needed. It does not itself promise credits, retroactive plan
changes, or courtesy waivers. Escalate to finance operations when a managed
service line cannot be reconciled to internal usage records, when the disputed
period spans more than one quarterly statement, or when the customer reports a
regulatory deadline that requires a documented answer sooner than the standard
review window.
